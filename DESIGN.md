# Design

## Theme

The page is one board under one sky. The hero is a pixel weather sim (cloud deck, rain, lake, falls) and everything under it is laid out like Mike's home base: hairline columns across the whole width, a small grey label with its rule, then a sentence that states a fact with one phrase lit. The theme picker is the sky's: **noon** (`light`) is the white under a noon sky, a hair cool, with a field of moss, sky and sand lights; **dusk** is a warm plum ground lit from the sky's own peach and indigo; **night** (`dark`) is home base's green-black, with moss and night-sky light pooling in the field. Picking a look pours a puff of that look's dust from under the button and wakes the sand, so the theme switch is a move in the toy, not a settings change.

Raspberry is the accent, not a flood: links, grains, the lit phrase, selection, small fills. Never a flat raspberry surface (Mike vetoed the wall of raspberry; the colour works grainy, not flat).

Strategy: **committed via the sand.** Raspberry carries identity through the hero word, the lit phrases, the grains and the links.

### What gradients and grain are allowed, and why

The old rule was "no gradients anywhere". The board needs a lit ground (home base's field), so the rule is now narrower:

- **The field** (`.field` in `board.css`) may use gradients: a fade from the page colour over the first 420px, so the seam under the masthead is invisible, then four large radial lights. All of it is one element's backgrounds, held still, scrolling with the page.
- **Noon's lit phrase** is a hard-stop linear gradient, a highlighter stroke through the lower half of the words. After dark the phrase turns raspberry instead, because a pale band on a dark ground reads as a selection.
- **Grain** is a 192px PNG per look with its strength baked into the alpha (`public/grain-light.png`, `grain-dark.png`), layered as a background of the same element. Never a translucent overlay of its own.
- Still never: gradients on text, buttons, cards or screenshots; animated gradients; `background-attachment: fixed`; full-viewport fixed or translucent overlays; animated `filter` or backdrop blur outside the hero's small pills. Mike's Firefox composites without a GPU, and any of these repaints a full-screen layer every scrolled frame. The cursor trail's canvas is fixed but removed from the page between strokes for the same reason.

Canvas sand palettes (grain shades, oklch strings fed to canvas):
- raspberry sand: L 0.54–0.66, C ~0.21, H 357
- amber sand: L 0.72–0.82, C ~0.14, H 75
- water: L 0.60–0.68, C ~0.13, H 250
- wall/ink grains: L 0.2–0.3, C 0.01, H 357

## Typography

- **Sora Variable** (100–800) for everything: display at 800 with -0.03em tracking, body at 400. Single family with hard weight contrast is the system. (Easter egg: Sora is also his dog's name; the colophon says so, and she lives in the sky column.)
- **Martian Mono Variable** for small metadata only: stacks, dates, captions, counts, the colophon. Never for body copy.
- Scale: h1 `clamp(2.6rem, 6.5vw, 5rem)`; shelf ledes `clamp(1.7rem, 2.1vw, 2.9rem)` at 640; tile lines `clamp(1.4rem, 1.5vw, 2.1rem)` at 600; body `1.0625rem/1.65`, board prose `0.96rem/1.62` in `--ink-2`.
- **Sentence headlines.** Every shelf and every project opens on one sentence with one phrase lit (`<em>` inside `.tile-line`, `.shelf-lede`, `.mast-take`), so the labels and the lines alone tell the whole page.
- All copy is lowercase by design (brand voice), including headings, project names and other people's products (t3 code, spotify, stripe) and alt text. Code identifiers keep their own case (appendChild). A test in `src/lib/showcase.test.ts` holds the project copy to it.
- `text-wrap: balance` on headings, `pretty` on prose. Prose max 58–62ch.

## Components

- **weather-hero**: full-bleed canvas band (`clamp(380px, 62vh, 640px)`), falling-sand engine under a pixel weather sim. "mike" stamped in raspberry+amber sand on a rock island in a lake. The word is kinetic sand: every grain is packed (a flag bit the automaton never moves) so hovering does nothing to it, the brush digs it loose in a ring around the pour, and a grain whose support is dug out lets go, so an undermined letter collapses upward the way sand does instead of floating. Rain erodes it: a drop landing on the word has an 8% chance to wash the grain under it off to an adjacent air cell, a puddle sitting on it 0.04% per tick, so edges nibble first and dunes form at the letters' feet. Water standing over sand (packed, or the loose grains washed into the crook of the k) drinks away at 0.6% per cell per tick, so a downpour leaves a wet crook and not a lake; only water over the rock floor is the lake. The erosion odds scale with the word's height so a phone's small word weathers at about the same pace. Pace at desktop: roughly a quarter of the word gone after three minutes of weather. Birds: a small flock (`src/lib/flock.ts`), six at noon, three at dusk, none at night, fewer under heavy cloud, drawn last as three- or five-cell silhouettes with a four-frame wing cycle and the odd glide. Now and then one lands on a letter (at most two sitting at once), head bobbing, and takes off at a raindrop, lightning, or the brush coming within twelve cells. Clouds are fbm noise quantised to three tones with a Bayer dither; dense cells rain, rain fills the lake, the lake spills off both ends of the island and falls past the intro copy (water is a conserved budget, so it cycles). Material chips (raspberry / amber / water / rock / moss / cloud / snow / lightning / erase) + reset in a pill bottom-left, swatches only under 720px so nine chips stay one row and off the word (cloud paints into the deck instead of the sand: a soft disc of density that drifts with the weather, rains through the shower gate on its own, and spends itself with every drop, so a stroke is about a minute of local shower; lightning is a press, not a stroke: a bolt drops from the cloud base over that column, or from the top of the sky when it is clear, and where it hits sand it does what lightning does to sand. Fulgurite first: the grains along the strike fuse into a pale aqua glass, a tube about twelve cells deep that wanders and thickens like the bolt, crosses the gap from the dot of the i to its stem, and stops at rock, water or ink. Glass is static and rain has nothing to wash off it, so a struck letter keeps a spine. Then the blast: every grain within four cells of the hit comes loose, most of it thrown up and out to fall back as a spray, the rest caving in after it. The weather's own night bolts do the same at a third of the size, so a storm leaves nicks and slivers of glass. A bolt that comes down on snow or ice melts everything frozen within five cells (two for the weather's own), and the blast around a sand hit melts what it reaches. Moss (`src/lib/moss.ts`) is the living layer: the chip drops a pinch of seed grains that fall like sand, and a perched bird leaves one behind now and then. A seed that comes to rest on soaked sand takes; on dry sand it waits for rain. Sand beside water is soaked and dries over about 25 seconds, and moss spreads from a soaked patch onto sand it touches, upward for preference, wicking onto dry sand from a wet patch, so after a shower the caps of the letters green first and vines creep down the sides. Moss is static and rain washes nothing off it, so a mossy edge stops eroding and the grain under it is bound; it is capped at 30% of the word so it crowns the letters and never carpets them; moss with nothing left around it drops off; lightning burns it). The moss brings company. At night, once the birds have gone, fireflies (`src/lib/fireflies.ts`) come out over it, one per ten cells of moss up to nine, each drifting around a patch of it on a curling wander and pulsing on its own clock, drawn as a warm yellow cell with a faint cross of halo mixed over whatever is under it. Three or more of them bring a frog (`src/lib/frog.ts`): a toad really, rust body and sandy head so it shows against the moss, set down on a letter near the moss. It sits, hops a few cells now and then (toward the fireflies more often than not, never off a drop deeper than four), and when a firefly comes within four cells of its head the tongue goes out, a pink line to the bug, and the bug is gone. Ten seconds with no fireflies (or ten afloat, if it did fall in the lake) and it hops off toward an edge. A snail (`src/lib/snail.ts`) turns up once there are fifteen cells of moss: amber shell, pale body, a horn up when it is not chewing. It crawls the surface of the letters at a step every nine ticks, climbs a one-cell step, lets itself down a drop of up to four, turns from anything deeper, wades a puddle and sinks through the lake to crawl its floor, and stops to graze any moss under or ahead of it back to packed sand, a mouthful taking fifty ticks. Half a minute without a mouthful and it wanders off an edge, or is simply gone after ten seconds of trying. Fish (`src/lib/fish.ts`) come with the water: one per sixty lake cells that have water over and under them, up to three, fading in one at a time and fading out as the lake goes down (with slack, so a lake at the margin does not blink them). Orange, two cells and a flicking fin, drawn a shade under the water and hidden behind a letter's overhang; they cruise the basin at a step every eight ticks, turn at the banks, keep a cell of water over their backs, and now and then one leaps clear on an arc that carries it six cells, only where the arc comes down in water rather than on a letter. A frozen-over lake keeps them under the ice. Snow (`src/lib/frost.ts`) is a cold cloud: the chip paints chill into the deck alongside density, and a raining cell over a chilled column lets go a flake instead of every other drop. Flakes are not grains: they drift at a tenth of a cell per tick with a sway and a lean into the wind, pass falling rain, and land on the first thing under them, capping sand, rock, moss or glass with a cell of snow; a flake over standing water freezes it to ice, and while it snows the ice creeps sideways across the lake at 2% per cell every four frames, so a long shower sheets the lake over. Snow and ice are static and shed rain. The sun takes them back: an open-faced cell of either turns to water at 0.6% every four frames scaled by how far the sun is above 0.35 and by clear sky, so at noon the caps go in half a minute and soak the word for the moss, at dusk they barely soften, and at night nothing melts. Flakes, snow and ice all count in the water budget, so a snowed-in word means a shallower lake until it thaws; the three looks in a pill top-right (swatches only under 720px); a mono readout pill bottom-right: "touch the sand" until woken, then humidity and cloud cover (under 720px the readout moves top-left and shows only the nudge until the first touch, then only the numbers). The pills are frosted glass: half-transparent page background over a 14px backdrop blur, hairline edge at 16% ink, a thin highlight along the top, so each look tints them and the clouds smear through. Solid page background where backdrop-filter is unsupported. The island dissolves into the page over its last 12 rows with a two-tone dither whose tones sink toward the page together, so the seam is a texture and never a checkerboard on white. No glow pass: night grains are moonlit by an ambient multiply, not bloomed.

## Layout

- The masthead keeps clear of the falls: its width is the hero's minus both shafts (`--shaft`, written by the sim after hydration; until then `--shaft-guess` in `styles.css` does the sim's sum in css, so the first paint is the final box). The sky's sentence shares a grid cell with an invisible copy of the longest thing it will say, so it never grows when the first reading lands, and the Sora face has a metric-matched local fallback (`Sora Fallback`) and a preload, so the lede wraps the same before and after the font arrives. CLS on load is about 0.001 at all four sizes. Three columns from 1840px; letter and sky with the index under them on a laptop, compacted to a one-line legend and the twelve names flowing on the next line, so the first shelf's headline lands within about a screen and a half at 1440x900; one column on a phone, where the index goes (the doors in the letter do its job) and the sky shrinks to Sora and one line, so the first project's line lands in the second screen.
- The board fills the width with `--pad` (`clamp(14px, 1.6vw, 40px)`) at the edges and `--col` (`clamp(18px, 1.9vw, 44px)`) inside each hairline column. Breakpoints: 900px (story beside screen, spreads), 1200px (nákup's phones beside its toy) and 1840px (screens big enough to read, pairs side by side, nákup in one row).
- Width goes to placing things side by side and to drawing screens bigger; a group (a shot and its caption, a story and its links) stays tight; prose keeps its measure.
- `/hire` breaks out into its own `min(100% - clamp(3rem, 8vw, 10rem), 140rem)` page: letter column plus work column.
- No scroll-reveal kit: nothing on the board slides or fades in as a block. The few entrances that exist (the Motion section) are the sand's own gestures on small things, and every box is in its final place before anything moves.

## Motion

The page is one world with the sand: things pour, settle, tick and respond the way the hero does, once each, and nothing loops for attention. The rule under all of it: motion starts from the element's final box (no layout ever shifts for it), it is `transform` and `opacity` on small elements, and it runs once. A visitor should notice the page is alive, never wait for it, and never lose their place to it.

- **The simulation** is the centerpiece motion; it runs only after a touch and pauses off-screen.
- **Entrances, once, on first scroll-in** (`src/lib/arrival.ts`, `useArrival`): an element is `settled`, `waiting` or `arriving`, written on it as `data-arrival`. The server, a visitor without javascript and anyone who asked for reduced motion only ever see `settled`, the final state. An element already on screen when the page lands is left alone (no blink on hydration); one below the fold waits, arrives once when it scrolls in, and is never watched again.
  - **Grain charts pour** (`pixel-charts.tsx`, `grain-pour` in `board.css`): each grain falls from above the svg's frame and lands where it belongs, bottom row first, sweeping across the days or the stations; the svg clips the fall so a grain appears at the top edge already moving. 340ms per grain with an ease-in (gravity, no bounce), a chart done in about a second. The font maps fall a row at a time, since their cells are two pixels and there are hundreds. Each grain carries its own delay as `--d`.
  - **The lit phrase lights** (`Lit` in `board.tsx`, `phrase-light`): noon's highlighter stroke sweeps through the words left to right (`background-size`), after dark the words warm from the ink to the raspberry (`color`), 560ms, a beat after the line lands. One rule for all three looks: `--mark` is the stroke (transparent after dark) and `--lit` the words' colour (the ink at noon).
  - **The try-it grain lands** (`mark-land` in `lazy-mini.css`): the raspberry square before "try it" drops and squashes once when its caption scrolls in, the gesture the h1's full stop already makes on load.
- **The sky column is live, not swapped** (`sky-report.tsx`): the strip's columns are keyed by reading, so a new reading's grains are new elements that fall into the right edge (the same pour, 220ms) and the column before them turns from raspberry to water where it stands (`fill` transition). When Sora's mood changes (rain starts, the look turns to night) the new portrait settles in over the old one and her line fades through, 420ms; the first render shows her plainly. The live dot does not pulse: a beat every 350ms would be a loop for attention, and the strip already is the heartbeat.
- **The viewer opens out of the screenshot and closes back into it** (`lightbox.tsx`, `flipFrom`): one transform on the dialog's image from the page figure's box to its fit box and back (220ms), while the room and the chrome fade in the same beat. The dialog itself never fades, so the shot is solid from the first frame. A shot that was zoomed, or stepped away from the one that opened, has nowhere on the page to go and fades instead. Escape is intercepted so it closes the same way. Zooming to actual pixels stays instant.
- **A change of look eases across the board's ground** (`.field` in `board.css`, `@property` in `styles.css`): the field copies `--bg` and its four lights into properties registered with `inherits: false` and transitions those, so a frame of the ease restyles one element. The ground keeps pace with the canvas's own ground (250ms) and the four lights come last at 650ms, the way the sky above takes its time. Text, rules, accents and the hero's chrome take the new look at once. An earlier version eased the shared tokens on `body`, which restyled every element on the page every frame and, with the sim running at the top of the page, dropped Firefox without GPU layers to 30–45 fps. The grain png swaps.
- **The toys answer a press with a little weight**: beadside's answers and send sit down a pixel and come back (120ms), tally's session rows the same, the model map's ring glides from dot to dot (160ms) instead of jumping, nákup's chips already pressed. The toys are otherwise still until touched; they mount about a screen before they scroll in and get no entrance of their own, since nobody would see it.
- **Micro-interactions**: 160–220ms, ease-out only (link arrows lean out, underlines lift, index entries nudge).
- Outbound links get their `↗` from one rule in `styles.css` (`main a[href^='http']::after`); markup never types one. A link that is a picture or carries its own arrow opts out with `.no-arrow`.
- **Never**: animated `filter`, `backdrop-filter`, `box-shadow` or large-area `clip-path`; full-viewport overlays that animate (the viewer's backdrop fade is the one exception, once on open and once on close, both set off by the visitor, and it predates this); anything that repaints a layer every scrolled frame; canvas work that runs when it is not visible. Mike's own Firefox composites without a GPU.
- **Performance check** (2 Oct 2026, production build in headless software-rendered Chrome at 2336x1400, rAF frame times): the chart pour and the phrase light drop no frames at 1x–3x CPU throttle; the viewer's open and close cost a few long frames around the bigger rung's decode (same as the plain fade did); scrolling is unchanged. A change of look was then re-measured in headless Firefox 150 with `layers.acceleration.disabled` at 2336x1400 (`/tmp/portfolio-verify` rig, interleaved runs on a loaded machine): with the field-local ease it costs the same as no ease at all, 52 fps median at the top of the page (where the sim and the hero's own change of look share the frame) and 60 mid-page, against 28–49 at the top with the old `body` token ease.
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: the page is complete and calm. Every `data-arrival` is `settled` from the first paint, the simulation never runs (a click still stamps sand), the sky column says so, the cursor trail is off, the viewer opens and closes in a frame, the look flips at once, and every transition and animation collapses to nothing. Only what the visitor directly drags moves.
